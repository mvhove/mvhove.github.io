To be myself

June 30th, 2023

I’ve thought a lot over what form this should take and what words I should use but I suppose it is best to just get to the point. I’m a trans woman. My methods of mitigating this were such as to where this will either be a complete shock or absolutely unsurprising. Either way, I came out to my parents in the past day and I wish to do the same publicly now.

By its very nature, I understand that for the vast majority of those reading this, the very concept of feeling this way is incomprehensible. And honestly, I don't really have the words to describe it. But what I can say is that I have known this about myself for a very long time and yet it has taken every ounce of my being to choke out the words. I physically couldn’t for a solid minute of trying. It came out as an almost whisper. To even put this in writing the next day stings at some level. It is me at a certain level of vulnerability I have spent my entire life trying to avoid. And yet, every bone in my body compels me to continue.

I would have given everything I own and every dollar I will ever make to not feel this way about myself. I resisted accepting who I am in every way imaginable. I looked to alleviate some of the symptoms while maintaining the visage of the person everyone sees. I asked myself how I could be so successful socially, politically, romantically, if these feelings were truly so crippling. I regarded the dysphoria I felt as lesser than that of the trans people around me, and therefore the actions needed to care for it less drastic.

For all I believe and have always believed, I refused to apply it to myself. Partly out of a great deal of shame, partly out of a desire to be who those around me pushed me to be, and partly out of fear. Fear of losing many people I care about permanently. Fear of being regarded as subhuman by a great deal of contemporary society. And even fear of simply being wrong in ascribing these feelings to gender instead of say depression or personal trauma.

I could litigate each and every detail of how I came to this definite conclusion about myself, but to do such would require far more words and distract from the point of this letter. I will have a lifetime to ask myself why when I came to this conclusion about myself, I tried to repress it. Perhaps what has kept me in the closet the longest has been the knowledge that in this moment, I must face that the past few years of my life could have been of great joy and discovery rather than simply treading water, and at times failing to.

Denial is a powerful placebo. When you are running, you really do feel as if you can go on forever. But invariably there comes a time where your legs simply give out. You crumble to the ground and as you look back at what you left in your wake, you realize it is all far more reflective of your inner turmoil than the normalcy you hoped to project. You simply find that there is a ceiling to your ambitions, to your abilities that you cannot shatter while ignoring such a crucial part of yourself.

I view the general purpose of life to be in each of our own ways trying to make this world a marginally better place. I know I cannot do that as I live today. I feel I owe it to myself past, present, and future to not be bound by this ceiling. This is for me the first step of what will be in many ways a lifelong process. I expect people to make occasional mistakes. I expect some to need time. But what I do ask is that as I begin this journey, that I may be granted your courtesy, respect, patience, and I will do my best to reciprocate it.

Some of my paranoias will undoubtedly come true. The moment this letter is published I will lose many people in my life. I do not pretend that that won’t deeply discomfort me. And yet I have done enough tightrope-walking in these past few years. I am who I am. I will be here with open arms for those that need time, be it a day, a week, or years. And if they never do, I will not forget the good times we spent together on the sole basis of their rejection.

I would be remiss to not mention the environment in which we live. I have always been a political person, but I’ve been very privileged that until this point my identity has not been in the crossfire. This is not a political manifesto. I write more than enough of those. But what I do wish to say is that I believe a lot of the current sentiment boils down to fear of the unknown. Fear of undue cultural influence. Issues of identity are hard to discuss exactly because mainstream acceptance of LGBT people is new and these identities deeply personal. Even questioning edge-issues can often trigger a kneejerk defensive response because they implicitly invoke the not-so-distant past where there was little to no social acceptance of trans people.

I don’t believe that those people are my enemies. I believe we as humans have a phenomenal capacity to evolve in our beliefs. I believe mere exposure to people like me is the best way to move forward as a country. When trans people are relegated to the domains of movies, cable news, and dehumanized political discourse, we are easy to stereotype and see as some distant phenomenon. We are easy to disregard as something your friend will never be, or your child will never be, or your grandchild will never be. Not so much when you know one. Part of why I do this today is in this political moment, I want to be that person for a lot of people who don’t have a trans person on even the fringes of their life. I want to be visible and open in order to promote a greater sense of understanding and empathy, even if only at the micro level I am capable of influencing.

I would like to thank those who have in their own ways guided me to this moment. They are too numerous to mention individually. Some remain in my life, others have departed from it. They have been everyone from acquaintances, to friends, to girlfriends, to basically just passers-by. They have been people to bounce questions off of, shoulders to cry on, and people to look up to. Some of them have no idea they helped me, some of them knew more about who I was than I did at the time. They were invaluable, and I owe them more for their assistance than any words I can string together will match.

I know for a fact many people reading this are transgender too and for one reason or another are not out. I know this both because some of you have privately told me and the sheer mathematical reality that about 5% of my generation is trans. Perhaps some of this has resonated with you. Perhaps you do not know yourself fully as I didn’t. Perhaps you fear how your parents and friends will respond. All that I will say to you today is there has not been a moment in my life thus far where I have felt as much overwhelming joy as I do at this very second.


With pride,
